# Anti-Vision Process — Competitive & Demand Research

Date: 2026-08-23. Question asked: does anything already do this, is there demand, and which vertical should it be sold into?

---

## 1. What already exists

### Direct — same core mechanic ("name what you don't want first")

| Product | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **AntiVision: Change Course** (antivisionapp.com) | Consumer app. You write "an ordinary Tuesday in 2036 if nothing changes"; the app resurfaces it verbatim later. | Closest named competitor. Single-mechanic app: aversion as a recurring nudge. No mechanism-extraction, no inversion, no weekly test. Site is behind an egress block here — worth a manual look. |
| **The Counter-Vision Blueprint** (Gumroad) | Paid PDF/worksheet: "What don't you want?" → clear out what doesn't belong. | Info-product, one-person operation. Proves people pay for the framing, not that they pay much. |
| **Anti-vision essays** (Substack: intentionalview, ateamfit, and many others) | Free written walkthroughs of the same idea. | The *concept* is in circulation and has an audience. The *process* is not productized. |
| **Mindsera** | AI journal built around named mental models — First Principles, Ikigai, **Regret Minimization**. | The strongest structural analogue: framework-as-product. Regret minimization is anti-vision's cousin. |

**Read:** the idea is not novel and is not owned. Nobody has built the full five-step loop (dump → mechanism → invert → testable → rank) with re-runs and longitudinal comparison. That last part — "mechanisms that show up in every round are the structural ones" — is the only genuinely defensible piece, and no competitor has it.

### Adjacent — competing for the same "I'm stuck" moment

- **AI journaling:** Rosebud (founder previously built Secret, 15M users), Reflection.app (100+ guided therapeutic programs, real-time AI coaching), Mindsera, Life Note. Crowded, well-funded, retention-hard.
- **Values clarification, clinical:** ACT values card sorts everywhere — Therapist Aid, SimplePractice (free download into the EHR), therapistassist.app (freemium: 1 client / 5 sends per month free). Same job-to-be-done, already free, already inside clinician workflow.
- **Worksheet economy:** Etsy and TeachersPayTeachers sell values-sort PDFs for a few dollars. This is the floor price of a framework with no software around it.
- **Coaching delivery software:** CoachAccountable ($20/mo at 2 clients → $4,000/mo at 1,000, worksheets + tracked actions + sponsor-visible engagement reports), Paperbell ($47.50–57/mo, sells and delivers packages), Practice ($50–75/mo). These are pipes, not content — they do not supply the exercise.
- **Team version:** the pre-mortem. Miro/Miroverse and the Atlassian playbook have this covered for projects, free, inside the tool teams already own. Do not fight this.

### The enterprise buyers of this outcome

- **Coaching platform market:** ~$4.23B in 2026 → $12.22B by 2036 (11.2% CAGR). Career coaching ≈ 28% of coaching type.
- **Career education/counseling:** $2.84B (2025) → $3.05B (2026).
- **AI-driven career coaching tools:** projected ~$500M by 2027.
- **Outplacement:** ~$5.6B growing to ~$10B by 2035. Randstad RiseSmart publishes per-person tiers: **$499 / $899 / $1,899 / $2,499**.
- **BetterUp** publishes 19% burnout reduction / 38% languishing reduction at 3 months — the outcome language any B2B buyer expects.

## 2. Is there demand?

**Yes for the outcome, weak for the artifact.** Three levels:

1. **Search/attention demand — high.** "Figure out what I want," career-clarity journaling, values prompts: enormous evergreen content volume, and a January/quarterly seasonal spike. Cheap to reach, hard to monetize — the audience expects free.
2. **Willingness-to-pay, consumer — low-to-medium.** The comparable price points are a $5 Etsy worksheet and a ~$10/mo AI journal with brutal churn. A standalone consumer anti-vision app competes with a free Substack post. AntiVision app existing and being unremarkable is evidence of the ceiling, not of a moat.
3. **Willingness-to-pay, professional/institutional — high.** Corporate burnout-coaching engagements run $5K–$25K+. Physician replacement costs $500K–$1M+, burnout rates 46–58%, and small-group coaching cuts burnout ~30% (vs ~13.5% for 1:1 and +11% in controls). Outplacement is $499–$2,499 per head. These buyers pay for a *structured, repeatable, measurable* intervention — which is exactly the shape of this process and exactly what a Substack post isn't.

**Conclusion:** the process is worth little as a consumer app and a lot as a professional instrument. Sell the instrument, not the insight.

---

## 3. Where the gap actually is

Everything on the market is either (a) a prompt with no structure, or (b) structure with no continuity. Nothing does:

- **Mechanism extraction** — the "why does that bother me" x3 collapse from 30 items to 6–8 mechanisms. This is step 2, it's where the value is, and it's the step that most needs guidance (AI is genuinely good at it).
- **Falsifiability** — "true or false by Friday" turns feelings into a checkable weekly item. Nothing in the journaling category produces a testable artifact.
- **Longitudinal mechanism tracking** — run 6 times over 6 months, show which mechanisms recur every round. This is a data asset that compounds per user and cannot be replicated by a PDF, a Miro board, or a one-shot chat.
- **A third-party view** — a coach/clinician/HR sponsor seeing the client's mechanisms and weekly pass/fail without reading their journal. Privacy-preserving *signal* rather than diary access.

That fourth point is the product wedge. It's also what makes it B2B2C.

## 4. Vertical candidates, ranked

Scored on: pain, budget, reachability, fit with the process, and defensibility.

### 1 — Career/executive coaches running structured programs (best beachhead)
- ~Tens of thousands of solo coaches already pay $50–$400/mo for delivery software that supplies **no content**.
- They resell process. Their #1 unpaid labor is client intake — "what do you actually want?" — the exact thing steps 1–3 solve.
- Reachable directly (ICF communities, coach-training alumni lists, LinkedIn) with no enterprise sales cycle.
- Sell as: white-labelled client-facing tool, per-coach seat + per-active-client, sitting alongside CoachAccountable/Paperbell rather than replacing them. Target $49–$149/mo per coach.
- Risk: coaches are famously cheap and churn. Mitigate by pricing on active clients, not seats.

### 2 — Physician/clinician wellbeing (highest revenue per logo)
- Burnout 46–58%, replacement $500K–$1M+ per physician, budgets already exist and are already being spent on coaching, and there's published evidence that group coaching works.
- The process reframes burnout from an emotion into a scheduling/structure problem — exactly the framing a CMO/CWO can act on.
- Sell as: cohort program + dashboard to the Chief Wellness Officer, $15K–$60K per department per year. CME accreditation is a large unlock and a large lift.
- Risk: 9–18 month sales cycles, procurement, IRB/privacy scrutiny. Needs a design partner, not a launch.

### 3 — Outplacement / career transition providers
- $5.6B market, transparent $499–$2,499 per-participant pricing, and every major player is bolting on AI right now (CareerBuilder launched an AI outplacement product in 2025).
- Laid-off people genuinely cannot answer "what do you want next?" — anti-vision is a better first session than a resume review.
- Sell as: licensed module to INTOO/RiseSmart/LHH/ImpactGroup-type providers, or to HR directly during RIFs.
- Risk: you become a feature of someone else's contract; buyer is procurement-driven and slow.

### 4 — Therapists/ACT practitioners
- Perfect conceptual fit (values clarification is ACT's core), but the market is saturated with **free** tools and the clinical bar (HIPAA, liability, EHR integration) is high. Free is the competitor. Skip unless a clinical co-founder appears.

### 5 — Consumer app
- Do not lead here. AntiVision already occupies the name, journaling incumbents are funded, and the price ceiling is ~$10/mo against free Substack content. Keep a free consumer version as a top-of-funnel lead magnet for #1.

## 5. Recommendation

**Wedge: coaches. Expansion: clinician wellbeing. Consumer stays free as acquisition.**

Positioning to test: *"Your clients can't tell you what they want. This gets you a ranked list of their six real constraints in 45 minutes — and shows you, month over month, which ones never go away."*

The pitch to a coach is not "a journaling app." It's **structured intake + between-session evidence + a longitudinal record they can show a corporate sponsor** — the last of which is exactly what CoachAccountable already monetizes and cannot generate content for.

### Pricing to test
- Coach: $79/mo up to 10 active clients; $149/mo up to 30. Free for 1 client, forever (mirrors therapistassist.app's proven funnel).
- Org/clinician cohort: $12K–$40K per department per year, 20–100 participants, with an aggregate (non-identifying) mechanism dashboard.
- Consumer: free, with export-to-coach as the conversion event.

### What would kill it
- Coaches want to keep the framework proprietary and just use their own PDF. Test this first — it's the fastest disqualifier.
- Step 2 done badly by AI produces generic mush ("you value autonomy"), and the whole product collapses into another journaling app. Mechanism extraction quality *is* the product.
- Retention: a monthly-then-quarterly cadence means the app is idle most of the time. Continuity has to come from the weekly testable item, not the re-run.

### Next 2 weeks (validation, not building)
1. Twenty 20-minute calls with career/executive coaches. One question that matters: *"Walk me through the last time a client couldn't articulate what they wanted. What did you do, and what did it cost you in session time?"*
2. Run the process live, manually, for 5 paying clients of 2 coaches. Charge the coach $200 for the 5. Concierge, no code.
3. Ask the coach the disqualifier directly: *"Would you put your logo on this and hand it to clients before session one?"* If fewer than half say yes, the wedge is wrong — go to outplacement next, not consumer.
4. In parallel, one exploratory call with a hospital Chief Wellness Officer to size the cohort deal. Do not sell yet.
